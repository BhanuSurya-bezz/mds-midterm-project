01 · Research question

What customer patterns can we observe?

How do product categories, payment methods, device types, browsing activity, order values, and delivery experience vary across customer records?

02 · Prediction task

Can we classify returning customers?

The target variable is Is_Returning_Customer: 1 means returning and 0 means not returning. We compare multiple classifiers and evaluate performance on held-out data.

Evaluation metrics

  • Accuracy: overall share of correct predictions.
  • Precision: how often predicted returning customers are actually returning.
  • Recall: how many actual returning customers are identified.
  • F1-score: balance between precision and recall.
  • Confusion matrix: correct and incorrect predictions by class.

Initial observations

Descriptive facts from this dataset—not claims of causation.

Returning-customer label

2,990 of 5,000 records (59.8%) are labelled returning customers; 2,010 (40.2%) are labelled not returning.

Shopping patterns

Sports is the largest product category by record count (667). Credit Card is the most common payment method (2,012 records), and Mobile is the most common device type (2,795 records).

Browsing behavior

Average session duration is about 14.6 minutes, and the average number of pages viewed is about 9.0 per record.

Data quality

The supplied processed CSV contains 5,000 rows and 22 columns, with no missing values and no duplicate rows.

Conclusion

This project combines descriptive analysis with supervised classification to study e-commerce customer behavior. The analysis summarizes customer and order patterns, while model comparison tests how well different algorithms identify returning-customer records. Final model-performance conclusions should be reported using the actual held-out test results.
